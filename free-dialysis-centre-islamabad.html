Free Dialysis Centre in Islamabad

Haemodialysis for patients living with kidney failure, provided free at BJQ Hospital, Gulberg Greens, Islamabad.

In short: BJQ Hospital (Babar Javed Qureshi Hospital) in Gulberg Greens, Islamabad offers haemodialysis free of charge to patients who need it. Call or visit reception, bring your kidney reports and dialysis prescription, and the team will confirm your session schedule.

Who is the Free Dialysis Centre for?

The centre is for adults living with kidney failure (also called end-stage kidney disease) whose nephrologist has advised regular haemodialysis. It is part of our promise, Care For All: life-sustaining treatment should not depend on a family's ability to pay.

What is haemodialysis?

Haemodialysis cleans the blood when the kidneys no longer can. Blood flows from your body through a filter in the dialysis machine, which removes waste products, extra salt and fluid, and then returns to your body. A session usually lasts around four hours, and most patients need two or three sessions a week.

What to bring to your first visit

  • Your CNIC (and the patient's, if you are a family member registering on their behalf)
  • Recent kidney function and blood test reports
  • Your doctor's dialysis prescription or referral
  • A list of the medicines you take
  • Details of your dialysis access: fistula, graft or catheter

What happens during a session

  • A nurse checks your weight, blood pressure and general condition.
  • You are connected to the dialysis machine through your access point.
  • You rest in a comfortable chair while the machine filters your blood; many patients read, sleep or watch something.
  • At the end, the nurse disconnects you and checks you again before you go home.

Frequently asked questions

Is dialysis really free at BJQ Hospital?

Yes. BJQ Hospital runs a Free Dialysis Centre for patients who need regular haemodialysis. Contact reception to ask about registration and available session times.

How long does one dialysis session take?

Usually around four hours, though your nephrologist decides the exact length and frequency for you.

Can a family member stay with me?

Please ask reception about the current policy for attendants in the dialysis unit.
